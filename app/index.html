The problem

Prior authorization is insurance pre-approval: before an expensive scan or drug, the insurer must approve it or may not pay. It is slow, often done by fax and phone, and delays care. A US rule (CMS-0057-F) now requires insurers to handle it electronically, with decisions within 72 hours for urgent and 7 days for standard requests, from 2026–2027.

Key terms

  • FHIR: a standard format for health records, so hospital and insurer systems can exchange data. Think of it as a shared set of digital forms.
  • Resource: one type of form, filled in as JSON. Patient is who the person is, ServiceRequest is a doctor's order (e.g. "do an MRI"), Observation is a measurement (e.g. blood pressure), and Condition is a diagnosis.
  • Medical codes (LOINC, SNOMED CT, RxNorm, ICD-10): standard dictionaries, so "blood pressure" or "type 2 diabetes" means the same thing in every system.
  • Da Vinci CRD → DTR → PAS: the industry standard for electronic prior authorization, in three steps. Does this order need approval? → What paperwork is needed? → Submit the request and get the decision.

How this app works

Answering these questions needs three kinds of knowledge: the data format, the medical meaning and the insurer's rules. So a router model sends each question to the right expert models (FHIR structure, clinical terminology, prior authorization), each trained on its own domain on Nugen. They answer in parallel, and a synthesis model combines their answers. Your 👍 / 👎 feedback is used to find and fix mistakes, and a benchmark checks that each fix really helps.
