FHIR R4 Domain Reference: Conditions, Allergies and Medications

Condition

Condition records a clinical condition, problem, diagnosis, or other
health concern that has become relevant to a patient’s care. A Condition
can represent a diagnosis, a problem on a practitioner’s problem list,
or a concern requiring investigation or management.

Conditions are often connected to other resources. For example, a
Condition may be referenced as the reason for a MedicationRequest or
Procedure.

Observation vs Condition: A symptom or measurement that contributes to
establishing a diagnosis may be represented as an Observation. A
persistent problem, diagnosis, or clinical concern being tracked or
managed is generally represented as a Condition.

AllergyIntolerance

AllergyIntolerance records an individual’s propensity or risk of an
adverse reaction to a substance. It can represent allergies or
intolerances involving medications, foods, environmental substances,
biologic products, or other substances.

Important fields include: - clinicalStatus: for example active,
inactive, or resolved - verificationStatus: for example unconfirmed,
confirmed, refuted, or entered-in-error - type: allergy or intolerance -
category: food, medication, environment, or biologic - criticality: low,
high, or unable-to-assess - code identifying the substance or concept -
patient - reaction details

AllergyIntolerance is useful for clinical decision support and alerts
because an individual’s known allergies or intolerances can affect
treatment decisions.

MedicationRequest

MedicationRequest represents an order or request for medication and the
instructions for administration. It can be used for inpatient and
outpatient medication orders and can include the reason for the request.

MedicationRequest should be distinguished from: - MedicationDispense:
provision of medication supply - MedicationAdministration: an actual
administration of medication - MedicationStatement: a record that a
patient is taking or has taken a medication, including information
reported by a patient or clinician

A medication workflow can therefore be understood as: request/order ->
dispense -> administer while MedicationStatement records an assertion
about medication use.
