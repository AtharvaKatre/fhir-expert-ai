FHIR R4 Domain Reference: Observations and Diagnostic Results

Observation

An Observation is used to record measurements and simple assertions
about a patient, device, or other subject. It is central to clinical
data because observations support diagnosis, monitoring, establishing
baselines, and identifying patterns.

Examples include: - Blood pressure - Body temperature - Body weight -
Heart rate - Laboratory atomic results - Smoking status - Other
point-in-time clinical measurements

An Observation commonly contains: - status - category - code identifying
what was observed - subject - encounter - effective date/time - value,
when available - interpretation - reference ranges - notes

Observation should not normally be used as a substitute for a clinical
diagnosis. Diagnoses and longer-term clinical problems are generally
represented using Condition. Likewise, a complete laboratory report with
clinical context is better represented using DiagnosticReport, which can
reference individual Observation resources.

DiagnosticReport

DiagnosticReport represents the findings and interpretation of
diagnostic tests. It may include laboratory, pathology, microbiology,
hematology, imaging, and other diagnostic information.

A DiagnosticReport can contain: - the status of the report - the
patient/subject - the encounter - the diagnostic service category -
references to the request that initiated the investigation - references
to individual Observations - textual conclusions - coded conclusions -
references to images or media where applicable

A useful conceptual relationship is:

DiagnosticReport -> summarizes/interprets a diagnostic investigation ->
may reference one or more Observation resources -> provides a clinical
conclusion or interpretation

Example reasoning: If a patient has a laboratory result showing a
measured value, the individual measurement can be represented as an
Observation. A collection of related results with an overall
interpretation can be represented as a DiagnosticReport referencing
those Observations.
